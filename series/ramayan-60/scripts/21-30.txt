## 21 | Atri and Anasuya | A blessing for what was to come.
src: Valmiki, Ayodhya Kanda 117-119
[a peaceful forest hermitage by a stream] Deep in the forest, they visited the ashram of Sage Atri, one of the seven great sages of the universe.
[old Sage Atri welcoming Rama] Atri welcomed them like his own children, and led Sita to meet his wife.
[Anasuya, an aged woman glowing with spiritual power] Anasuya was very old, her hair white, her body thin from penance. Once, her prayers had made the Ganga flow through a land dying of drought, and had stopped ten years of famine.
[Anasuya welcoming Sita warmly] Atri's wife, Anasuya, a woman of great virtue, embraced Sita like a daughter.
[Anasuya giving Sita jewels and a garland] She was so pleased with Sita's devotion to Rama that she gave her divine gifts: a garland that never withers, clothes that never fade, and an ointment that would keep her beautiful forever.
[Anasuya advising Sita by lamplight] She spoke to Sita about devotion and strength, wisdom Sita would need in the days ahead.
[the three walking on at dawn] The next morning, the three walked on into the Dandaka forest, wearing those gifts.

## 22 | Viradha | The first demon. Not the last.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 1-4
[the sages of Dandaka welcoming Rama] At the edge of the Dandaka forest, the sages welcomed Rama with fruit and flowers. 'Protect us,' they begged. 'Demons hunt us here.'
[dark forest, a monstrous shadow] As they moved deeper into the forest, a terrible demon, Viradha, rose before them. He was as tall as a mountain, with a gaping mouth, wearing a tiger's skin.
[Viradha grabbing Sita] He snatched Sita and mocked the princes. 'You wear the clothes of hermits, but carry bows and a woman. I will make her my wife.'
[Rama and Lakshmana shooting arrows] Rama and Lakshmana's arrows flew again and again, but no weapon could stop him. He had a boon from Brahma.
[the brothers breaking his arms] So the brothers broke his arms and wrestled him to the ground.
[the princes burying him in a pit] At last, they buried him alive in a deep pit. Before dying, he revealed he was a cursed celestial named Tumburu, now free.

## 23 | Panchavati | Peace before the storm.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 11-15
[Rama visiting Sage Agastya's ashram] For ten years, Rama, Sita and Lakshmana wandered from ashram to ashram. Then they met the great Sage Agastya.
[Agastya pointing the way] He guided them to Panchavati, a lovely land beside the Godavari river.
[a beautiful riverside grove with five banyan trees] It was named for its five great banyan trees. Lotus ponds shone, and peacocks danced on the banks.
[Jatayu meeting them on the way] On the way, they met an old vulture, Jatayu, who said he was a friend of their father. 'I will guard Sita when you are away,' he promised.
[Lakshmana building a small hut] Lakshmana built a simple cottage of leaves and bamboo, with clay walls and a floor of sacred grass.
[the three sitting by the river at sunset] For a while, they lived in peace. Deer came close. Birds sang at dawn. Winter came, and Rama and Lakshmana bathed in the cold river at first light.
[Sita laughing, Rama smiling] These were the happiest days of their exile. But a storm was already on its way.

## 24 | Surpanakha | An insult that would burn Lanka.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 17-18
[a demoness watching Rama from the trees] One day, Ravana's sister Surpanakha saw Rama and fell in love. She had a pot belly, copper-colored hair and long claws for nails.
[her turning into a beautiful woman] She took a beautiful form and asked him to marry her. 'Leave this thin woman,' she said. 'I will eat her, and we will wander the forest together.'
[Rama gently refusing] Rama smiled and said, 'I am married. Ask my brother. He is alone here.'
[Lakshmana laughing] Lakshmana laughed and refused too. 'I am only a servant. Go back to Rama. He will make you his second wife.'
[Surpanakha realizing she is mocked] She realized they were mocking her. Her beautiful form fell away, and her true face burned with rage.
[Surpanakha attacking Sita in rage] Furious, she rushed at Sita in a rage.
[Lakshmana drawing his sword] Lakshmana stopped her with his sword and punished her, cutting her nose.
[Surpanakha running away screaming] Burning with hatred, she fled through the forest, howling, to her brothers.

## 25 | Khara and Dushana | One man. Fourteen thousand.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 19-30
[Surpanakha crying before Khara] Surpanakha fell weeping at the feet of her brother Khara, ruler of the demons in Janasthana, and begged for revenge.
[fourteen demons marching] Khara sent fourteen fierce warriors after Rama. Rama defeated all of them with fourteen arrows.
[a vast demon army, thousands strong] Then Khara and his general Dushana brought fourteen thousand demons to the forest, with chariots, elephants and weapons of every kind.
[evil omens: blood rain, jackals howling] Evil omens filled the sky. Red rain fell, and jackals howled at noon. But Khara would not turn back.
[Rama sending Sita and Lakshmana to a cave] Rama sent Sita and Lakshmana to a cave in the mountain. 'This fight is mine,' he said.
[Rama alone, bow drawn] He faced the whole army alone, wearing armor that shone like the sun. His arrows filled the sky.
[Rama killing Dushana and Trishira] Dushana fell. Then the three-headed Trishira. Then, at last, Khara himself.
[fallen army, silent field] By evening, the entire army lay still. The sages came out of hiding and showered Rama with flowers.

## 26 | Ravana and Maricha | A plan was set.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 31-42
[Lanka's golden palace at night] In golden Lanka, the ten-headed king Ravana sat on his throne, among his ministers.
[Surpanakha before Ravana in his court] Surpanakha burst into the court and showed Ravana what had been done to her. 'You sit here drinking while a man defeats your army!'
[Ravana's ten heads, angry] She described Sita's beauty and goaded him. 'She should be your queen, brother. Bring her to Lanka.'
[Ravana's flying chariot crossing the sea] Ravana flew across the ocean in his golden flying chariot, pulled by donkeys with demon faces.
[Ravana visiting Maricha's hut] He went to Maricha, the demon Rama had once thrown across the ocean, now living as a hermit.
[Maricha shaking in fear] 'Do not touch Rama,' Maricha begged. 'I have felt his arrow. Every tree in this forest looks like Rama to me.'
[Ravana threatening Maricha with his sword] But Ravana grew furious and threatened him. 'Help me, or face my anger.'
[Maricha agreeing in despair] Maricha agreed in despair. 'Either way, my end is near. Better that it comes from Rama.'
[the two flying toward Panchavati] Together they flew toward Panchavati.

## 27 | The Golden Deer | Maricha's trap.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 42-44
[a shimmering golden deer near the hut] One morning, Sita saw a deer with a golden coat and silver spots. Its horns shone like jewels, and its hooves were like blue sapphire.
[the deer grazing among flowers] It played near the hut, coming close, then running away, as if it wanted to be seen.
[Sita pointing, eyes bright] Sita was enchanted. 'Rama, please catch it for me! If it lives, we will take it home to Ayodhya. If not, its golden skin will be a seat.'
[Lakshmana frowning] Lakshmana was uneasy. 'Brother, no deer like this exists. It could be a demon's trick. It could be Maricha.'
[Rama taking his bow] But Rama could not refuse Sita. 'If it is Maricha, I will deal with him,' he said.
[Rama giving Lakshmana instructions] 'Guard her well,' he told Lakshmana. 'Do not leave her alone for a moment.'
[Rama following the deer into the forest] And he followed the deer into the forest.
[the deer leading him far away] The deer led him deeper and deeper, appearing and vanishing among the trees, far away from the hut.

## 28 | The Lakshman Rekha | The line that must not be crossed.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 44-45 (the drawn line comes from later retellings)
[Rama's arrow striking the deer] Far away, Rama's arrow hit the deer, and it turned back into Maricha, a giant demon falling to the ground.
[Maricha crying in Rama's voice] With his dying breath, he cried out in Rama's voice: 'Sita! Lakshmana! Help me!'
[Rama realizing the trick, running back] Rama understood the trick at once, and began to run back toward the hut.
[Sita panicking] At the hut, Sita heard the cry and begged Lakshmana to go. He refused. 'No one in the three worlds can harm Rama.'
[Sita speaking harsh words in fear] Mad with fear, she spoke harsh words. She accused him of wanting Rama dead. Lakshmana covered his ears.
[Lakshmana drawing a line around the hut] Hurt but obedient, he drew a line around the hut, as later tellings say. 'Do not cross this, whatever happens.'
[Lakshmana praying to the forest spirits] He bowed to the trees and the forest spirits, and asked them to protect her.
[Lakshmana leaving] Then he went to find his brother, looking back again and again.

## 29 | The Abduction | Sita is taken.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 46-49
[a sage at the door, begging] As soon as he left, an old sage with an umbrella and a water pot came to the hut, asking for alms.
[the forest going silent] The trees stopped moving. The river grew still. Even the wind was afraid.
[Sita holding the food, hesitating] Sita saw nothing wrong with the old man. She offered him food and water from inside the line, as later tellings say.
[the sage refusing, demanding she step out] He insisted she come out. 'A holy man cannot take alms from behind a line.' She stepped over the line.
[the sage revealing ten heads, Ravana] The sage turned into Ravana, king of Lanka, his ten heads blazing with anger.
[Ravana praising Lanka, Sita refusing] 'Be my queen,' he said, 'and rule golden Lanka.' Sita refused. 'You are a jackal. Rama is a lion.'
[Ravana's flying chariot, Sita crying] He seized her and flew into the sky in his golden chariot.
[Sita crying out to the trees and river] 'Tell Rama!' she cried to the trees, the river and the deer. Sita's cries echoed across the forest.

## 30 | Jatayu | A bird who gave his life for dharma.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 50-51, 67-68
[the great vulture on a tree] Jatayu, an old vulture and friend of Dasharatha, heard Sita's cries.
[Jatayu warning Ravana] 'I am sixty thousand years old,' he called, 'but I will not let you take her. Put her down, Ravana!'
[Jatayu diving at Ravana's chariot] Though weak with age, he attacked Ravana with all his strength.
[Jatayu tearing the chariot apart] He smashed the chariot and fought the demon king with beak and talons.
[Ravana cutting off his wings] But Ravana drew his sword and struck down the brave old bird. Jatayu fell.
[Sita's jewels falling from the sky] As Ravana flew on, Sita dropped her jewels, wrapped in her scarf, onto a mountain where five monkeys sat.
[Rama finding the dying Jatayu] Later, Rama and Lakshmana found the dying bird. He lived long enough to tell Rama everything.
[Rama holding the dying Jatayu] 'Ravana took her south,' he whispered, and breathed his last in Rama's arms.
[Rama lighting Jatayu's funeral fire] Rama performed his funeral like a son, and wept for him as he had for his father.
