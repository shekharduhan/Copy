## 1 | The Kingdom of Ayodhya | The Ramayan begins…
src: Valmiki, Bala Kanda 5-16
[golden palace by the river Sarayu at dawn] On the banks of the Sarayu stood Ayodhya, a city of peace and dharma, where no one ever went hungry.
[King Dasharatha on his throne] Its king, Dasharatha, had wealth, power and his people's love. He had three queens. But he had no son.
[the king alone on a moonlit balcony, worried] Who would carry the line of the Sun after him? The question kept the old king awake.
[Vasishtha advising the king] On the advice of his guru, Sage Vasishtha, he invited the great sage Rishyasringa.
[sages around a sacred fire] Together they performed the Putrakameshti Yagna, the sacred fire ritual for sons.
[a divine being rising from the flames with a golden bowl of kheer] From the fire rose a shining being, holding a golden bowl. 'Share this kheer among your queens,' he said.
[old white-bearded King Dasharatha handing a golden bowl of kheer to his three queens Kausalya, Kaikeyi and Sumitra] The king shared it between Kausalya, Sumitra and Kaikeyi.
[three queens smiling] Soon, all three queens were with child. And so began the story that would shape the world.

## 2 | Four Princes Are Born | A hero was growing…
src: Valmiki, Bala Kanda 18-19
[palace celebrations, flowers and drums] In the month of Chaitra, on the ninth day, Queen Kaushalya gave birth to Rama, the embodiment of dharma. Today that day is celebrated as Ram Navami.
[baby Rama glowing in a golden cradle] Kaikeyi gave birth to Bharata. Sumitra gave birth to twins, Lakshmana and Shatrughna.
[four boys playing in the palace garden] The four grew up inseparable. Lakshmana's heart belonged to Rama, and Shatrughna's to Bharata. Lakshmana would not even eat unless Rama was beside him.
[lessons at Vasishtha's gurukul] Under Sage Vasishtha they mastered the Vedas, archery, riding elephants and chariots, and the duties of a righteous king.
[young Rama helping a villager] Yet Rama was loved for more than his skill. Even as a boy he was kind to everyone, and he always spoke first, with a smile.
[Dasharatha watching his sons with pride] The old king looked at his four sons and felt that every one of his prayers had been answered. He did not know how soon the world would come asking for them.

## 3 | Vishwamitra and Tataka | Rama's first victory.
src: Valmiki, Bala Kanda 18-26
[dark forest, frightened sages] Demons were destroying the prayers of the sages. Every time a sacrifice began, they rained blood and flesh on the sacred fire.
[Vishwamitra arriving at Ayodhya's court] The great sage Vishwamitra came to Ayodhya for help. Dasharatha promised him anything he wished.
[Vishwamitra before Dasharatha] 'Send Rama with me,' said the sage. Rama was not yet sixteen. Dasharatha was heartbroken, and offered his whole army instead.
[Vasishtha calming the king] But Vasishtha reminded him: a king's word cannot be broken. So Rama and Lakshmana went willingly.
[princes walking behind the sage along the river] On the road, Vishwamitra taught them the mantras Bala and Atibala, so they would never feel hunger, thirst or tiredness, and then the secrets of divine weapons.
[Tataka charging out of the forest] Then came Tataka, a fearsome demoness with the strength of a thousand elephants, who had turned a whole land into a wilderness.
[Rama's arrow flying] Rama was reluctant to fight her, but the sage reminded him that a protector must protect the innocent. His arrow flew true, and the forest was free again.

## 4 | Siddhashrama | Maricha will return…
src: Valmiki, Bala Kanda 29-30
[sages at a yagna, fire glowing] At Siddhashrama, where Lord Vishnu himself had once meditated, Vishwamitra began a sacred yagna in silence.
[Rama and Lakshmana standing guard at night] Rama and Lakshmana guarded it for six days and nights without sleep, bows always strung.
[dark clouds, demons descending] On the last day, the altar burst into flames and the sky went dark. The demon Subahu and his general Maricha came roaring down, ready to pour blood onto the fire.
[Rama's bow drawn, glowing arrow] Rama did not panic. He fitted the Manava astra to his bow.
[Maricha flung across the ocean] It struck Maricha in the chest and hurled him a hundred yojanas, far across the ocean. Rama had chosen not to kill him.
[Subahu falling in flames] Then the Agni astra struck Subahu, and he fell. With the Vayu astra, Rama swept the rest of the demons away.
[Maricha alone on a distant shore, trembling] But far away, Maricha woke up on a lonely shore, shaking. He would never forget that arrow.

## 5 | Ahalya | Rama's grace frees all.
src: Valmiki, Bala Kanda 48-49 (the stone form comes from later retellings)
[a silent, ruined hermitage] On the road to Mithila, they found an empty ashram, silent for centuries. No bird sang there.
[Vishwamitra telling the tale] 'This was the home of Sage Gautama,' said Vishwamitra. 'His wife Ahalya was the most beautiful woman the creator ever made.'
[Indra in disguise at the hermitage] 'One day, Indra came disguised as Gautama, and Ahalya was deceived. When the sage returned, he cursed them both.'
[Ahalya fading into the shadows] 'She would live here unseen, living on air, doing penance, until Rama came.' Many later tellings say she was turned to stone.
[Rama stepping in, light spreading] As Rama entered, the curse broke. Ahalya appeared, radiant and free, shining like the moon behind clouds.
[Rama and Lakshmana touching her feet] Rama and Lakshmana bowed and touched her feet, as one honors a mother.
[Ahalya bowing, flowers falling from the sky] She bowed in gratitude. Flowers fell from the sky, and divine drums sounded.
[Rama walking on toward Mithila] Rama had not fought anyone, yet he had won his gentlest victory. Her long penance had ended.

## 6 | Mithila and Shiva's Bow | Destiny was closing in.
src: Valmiki, Bala Kanda 50, 66-67
[the grand city of Mithila] King Janaka of Mithila welcomed the princes with honor. He was a king and a sage at once, wise and gentle.
[a giant bow on an iron cart pulled by many men] In his palace lay Shiva's mighty bow, so heavy that it took hundreds of men to pull it on its eight-wheeled cart. Not even gods and demons could lift it.
[kings failing to lift the bow] Many kings had come for it. Not one could even raise it off the ground.
[Janaka making his announcement] Janaka had made a vow: whoever strings this bow will marry my daughter, Sita.
[Vishwamitra smiling at Rama] Vishwamitra turned to Rama and smiled. He knew exactly why he had brought Rama here.
[Rama and Lakshmana looking at the bow in awe] Rama looked at the bow and said quietly, 'May I touch it?' Janaka nodded, and the whole court held its breath.
[the bow's box being opened, golden light] Now the great box was opened. Rama stepped closer, and the whole hall fell silent.

## 7 | The Birth of Sita | A daughter of the Earth.
src: Valmiki, Bala Kanda 66 (the casket and the bow as a toy are from later retellings)
[Janaka ploughing a field at sunrise] Years earlier, King Janaka was ploughing a field himself to prepare the ground for a sacred ritual.
[the plough striking something in the earth] Suddenly, the plough struck something hard in the soil.
[a golden casket rising from the furrow] From the earth emerged a glowing baby girl. Some tellings say she lay in a golden casket.
[Janaka lifting the baby] Janaka lifted her in his arms. He had no child of his own, and his heart filled with joy.
[the baby in the palace, flowers around her] He named her Sita, meaning 'furrow.' Born of the Earth, she was raised as his own daughter, and loved by all of Mithila.
[young Sita lifting the heavy bow with ease] Later tellings say that as a girl, she once moved Shiva's bow aside like a toy while cleaning the room.
[Janaka staring in wonder] Janaka saw it and knew she was no ordinary child. Only a man who could string that same bow would be worthy of her.

## 8 | The Swayamvar | Two souls, one destiny.
src: Valmiki, Bala Kanda 67-73 (Ravana at the contest is from later retellings)
[royal court, kings assembled] Kings from across the land came to win Sita's hand. One by one, they failed. Some could not even move it.
[Ravana pinned under the bow] In some later tellings, even mighty Ravana tried, and was crushed under the bow and left in shame.
[Rama rising calmly] Then Rama stepped forward. He bowed to his teacher, Vishwamitra, and walked to the bow.
[Rama lifting the bow with one hand] He lifted it as if it were light, and the whole court gasped.
[the bow snapping, thunder] As he drew the string to his ear, the bow broke in two, with a crack like thunder that shook Mithila. People fell to the ground in fear.
[Janaka rising in joy] Janaka rose, tears in his eyes. 'My vow is fulfilled. Sita will marry Rama.'
[Sita placing the garland] She placed the garland around Rama's neck, and the sky rained flowers.
[Rama and Sita's eyes meeting] Their eyes met for a moment. Ancient tellings say they were Vishnu and Lakshmi, finding each other again on earth.

## 9 | Four Weddings | Two kingdoms became one family.
src: Valmiki, Bala Kanda 68-73
[Janaka sending a message to Ayodhya] Janaka sent swift messengers to Dasharatha with the happy news.
[Dasharatha arriving with his court] Dasharatha arrived with his whole court, his army and his sages. The two kings embraced like brothers.
[Janaka with his daughters and nieces] Janaka made a joyful offer: his daughters and his nieces for all four princes.
[the brides side by side] Sita to Rama, Urmila to Lakshmana. Janaka's brother Kushadhwaja's daughters, Mandavi and Shrutakirti, to Bharata and Shatrughna.
[four couples at the fire] Four brothers married four sisters in one grand ceremony. They walked around the sacred fire together.
[Sita and Rama at the fire] As Janaka placed Sita's hand in Rama's, he said: 'She will follow you like your shadow.'
[gods showering flowers] Flowers fell from the sky, and divine drums sounded.
[Vishwamitra leaving for the Himalayas] His task complete, Vishwamitra blessed the couples and left for the Himalayas.
[Mithila celebrating] The whole city sang and danced for days. Janaka sent his daughters off with gold, silk, elephants and horses, and many tears.

## 10 | Parashurama | Rama's power was divine.
src: Valmiki, Bala Kanda 74-76
[the wedding procession on the road home] On the way back to Ayodhya, a storm rose. The earth shook, and birds cried out.
[Parashurama with his axe, eyes blazing] A fearsome sage blocked the path. It was Parashurama, who had wiped out proud kings twenty-one times. His axe was on his shoulder.
[Dasharatha begging for mercy] Dasharatha begged him to spare his sons. Parashurama ignored him.
[Parashurama holding out Vishnu's bow] 'You broke Shiva's bow,' he said to Rama. 'Now string Vishnu's bow, if you can.'
[Rama taking the bow calmly] Rama took the bow, strung it, and fixed an arrow in one smooth motion.
[Rama aiming at Parashurama] 'This arrow cannot go to waste,' said Rama. 'Shall I destroy your path, or the worlds you have won by your penance?'
[Parashurama bowing his head] Parashurama recognized the truth. He bowed, gave up his worlds to the arrow, and withdrew in peace.
[Parashurama's eyes softening as he recognizes Vishnu] Looking into Rama's eyes, Parashurama saw Lord Vishnu himself, and his anger melted away.
