## 31 | Kabandha | A clue in the darkness.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 69-73
[Rama and Lakshmana searching the forest] Rama and Lakshmana searched everywhere for Sita, calling her name through forests and mountains. Rama asked the trees, the rivers and the deer if they had seen her.
[a dark, tangled forest, no birds] They entered a forest so thick and dark that no sunlight reached the ground, and not a single bird sang.
[a huge headless demon with long arms] Suddenly, a giant demon named Kabandha seized them in his arms. He had no head or neck. His mouth was in his belly, and a single fiery eye glowed in his chest.
[Kabandha's arms stretching for miles] His arms stretched for miles, and he caught anything that walked into them, and ate it.
[the brothers cutting off his arms] Rama cut off the right arm, Lakshmana the left. Kabandha crashed to the ground and cried out in relief.
[Kabandha telling his story] 'I was a celestial named Danu,' he said. 'Indra struck me with his thunderbolt and drove my head into my body. Only you could free me.'
[a radiant being rising from the funeral fire] The brothers burned his body on a great fire. From the flames rose a radiant being, shining in a celestial chariot.
[the celestial pointing toward a mountain] He gave them a clue: 'Seek Sugriva, king of the monkeys, at Mount Rishyamukha. He knows Ravana's way. And first, visit Shabari.'

## 32 | Shabari | Devotion is the highest offering.
src: Valmiki, Aranya Kanda 74 (the tasted berries come from later retellings)
[an old woman sweeping a forest path] Near Lake Pampa lived Shabari, an aged devotee who had waited years for Rama. She was a tribal woman, born far from any palace.
[Shabari as a young girl leaving home] As a young girl, she had run away from home to serve the sage Matanga. Before he died, he told her: 'Wait here. One day, Rama will come.'
[Shabari growing old in the ashram] So she waited. Years became decades. Her hair turned white. Every morning, she swept the path and scattered flowers, in case today was the day.
[Shabari carrying a basket of berries] Each day she gathered the sweetest berries, tasting each one to be sure it was good, as later tellings lovingly say.
[Shabari seeing Rama arrive] And one day, two princes walked up the path. Shabari fell at Rama's feet, weeping.
[Rama eating the berries with joy] When Rama finally came, he happily ate the berries she had already tasted. Lakshmana hesitated. Rama did not.
[Shabari in tears of joy] He cared nothing for rank or custom. He saw only her love.
[Shabari rising into the sky in light] Shabari told them where to find Sugriva. Then, her life's wish fulfilled, she entered a sacred fire and rose to heaven.

## 33 | Hanuman Meets Rama | Hanuman finds his Lord.
src: Valmiki, Kishkindha Kanda 2-4
[Sugriva watching from a mountain, afraid] On Rishyamukha, the exiled monkey king Sugriva saw two warriors approaching with bows. He feared his brother Vali had sent them to kill him.
[Sugriva sending Hanuman] So he sent his wisest minister, Hanuman, son of the wind god, to find out who they were.
[Hanuman disguised as a humble brahmin] Hanuman came disguised as a priest. He spoke so wisely and gently that Rama turned to Lakshmana in wonder.
[Rama praising Hanuman's speech] 'He has spoken for so long, without a single mistake,' Rama said. 'Only one who has mastered the Vedas could speak like this.'
[Lakshmana explaining their story] Lakshmana told him everything: the exile, Sita, and Ravana.
[Hanuman revealing his true form, bowing] Hanuman revealed his true form and fell at Rama's feet, knowing instantly who he was. His heart overflowed.
[Hanuman lifting both brothers on his shoulders] He grew large, lifted both brothers on his shoulders, and leapt up the mountain.
[Hanuman carrying them to Sugriva] He carried them to Sugriva. A lifelong devotion had begun. From that day, Hanuman lived only for Rama.

## 34 | The Pact with Sugriva | Two exiles, one promise.
src: Valmiki, Kishkindha Kanda 5-8
[Rama and Sugriva before a sacred fire] Hanuman lit a sacred fire. Rama and Sugriva walked around it together and sealed their friendship. 'Your joy is my joy,' Rama said. 'Your sorrow is my sorrow.'
[Sugriva telling his story] Sugriva told his story. His brother Vali, king of Kishkindha, had driven him out, taken his kingdom and his wife Ruma.
[Vali fighting a demon in a cave, flashback] Once, Vali had chased a demon into a cave, and Sugriva waited outside for a year. When blood flowed out, he thought Vali was dead, and sealed the cave.
[Vali returning in rage] But Vali returned alive, and never forgave him. 'Help me,' Sugriva said, 'and my army will find Sita.'
[Rama nodding] Rama promised to defeat Vali.
[Sugriva showing Sita's jewels] Then Sugriva showed him the jewels Sita had dropped as she was carried away, wrapped in her yellow scarf.
[Rama holding the jewels and weeping] Rama held them and wept. He asked Lakshmana if he knew them.
[Lakshmana looking at the anklets] Lakshmana said: 'I do not know her earrings or bracelets. I only ever looked at her feet. These anklets are hers.'

## 35 | The Fall of Vali | Sugriva is king.
src: Valmiki, Kishkindha Kanda 11-26 (Vali's boon to take half his enemy's strength comes from later retellings)
[Rama splitting seven sal trees with one arrow] To prove his strength, Rama shot one arrow through seven giant sal trees, and it came back to his quiver.
[Sugriva challenging Vali at his gate] Sugriva challenged Vali to a duel. Vali, the mightiest monkey, was said to take half the strength of any enemy who faced him.
[two identical-looking monkeys fighting] The two looked alike, and Rama hesitated to shoot, fearing he might hit the wrong one. Sugriva fled, bleeding.
[Sugriva wearing a garland as a mark] The next day, Sugriva wore a garland of flowers as a mark.
[Tara warning Vali] Vali's wife Tara begged him not to fight. 'Sugriva has a new ally. Rama.' Vali did not listen.
[Rama's arrow flying from behind a tree] This time Rama's arrow flew true, from behind a tree, and struck Vali in the chest.
[Vali dying, speaking to Rama] Vali asked, 'Why strike from hiding?' Rama explained his duty: Vali had taken his brother's wife, and a king must punish that.
[Vali blessing his son Angada] Vali accepted, and asked Rama to protect his son, Angada. Sugriva was crowned king of Kishkindha.

## 36 | Waiting Out the Monsoon | The search begins.
src: Valmiki, Kishkindha Kanda 27-35
[heavy rains over the mountains] With the monsoon season, no army could march. Rama and Lakshmana waited in a cave on Mount Prasravana for four long months.
[rivers flooding, lightning over the forest] Rivers flooded, lightning cracked over the forest, and the roads disappeared under water.
[Rama gazing at the sky, alone] Every night, Rama grieved for Sita. The storms only made it harder. Every peacock's cry, every flash of lightning reminded him of her.
[Lakshmana comforting Rama] Lakshmana sat beside him. 'Grief steals strength,' he said. 'Hold on, brother. We will find her.'
[Sugriva enjoying luxury in his palace] Meanwhile, Sugriva fell into comfort, with music, wine and his queens, and forgot his promise.
[the rains ending, clear autumn sky] Autumn came. The sky cleared. But no army gathered.
[Lakshmana storming into the city, bow in hand] When the rains ended, an angry Lakshmana came to remind him. He twanged his bow, and the whole city shook.
[Tara calming Lakshmana] Wise Queen Tara came out first and calmed him. Sugriva was ashamed, and ordered every monkey army in the world to gather.

## 37 | The Search Begins | Hope was running out.
src: Valmiki, Kishkindha Kanda 37-53
[monkey armies arriving from every direction] Millions of monkeys and bears came from mountains, forests and oceans, covering the land like clouds.
[monkey armies leaving in four directions] Sugriva sent search parties to the east, west, north and south. 'Come back within one month,' he ordered, 'or face death.'
[Rama giving Hanuman his ring] Rama gave Hanuman his signet ring, engraved with his name. 'Show this to Sita. She will know you come from me.'
[the southern party led by Angada, Jambavan and Hanuman] The southern party, led by Prince Angada, with Jambavan and Hanuman, searched cave after cave.
[the monkeys trapped in a magical cave] They wandered into a magical underground cave and were lost there, until a holy woman, Swayamprabha, brought them out with her power.
[monkeys sitting hopelessly on a beach] When they came out, they were on the shore of the southern ocean. A month had passed. They had found nothing.
[Angada declaring he will fast to death] The deadline was gone. Despair settled in. Angada sat down to fast until death, rather than return in shame.
[the monkeys sitting in silence by the sea] One by one, the others sat beside him, facing the endless sea.

## 38 | Jambavan and Sampati | Who can cross the ocean?
src: Valmiki, Kishkindha Kanda 56-63, 65-66
[the great vulture Sampati, wingless on a cliff] On the shore, a huge old vulture crawled out of a cave. It was Sampati, brother of the fallen Jatayu. He had no wings.
[Sampati hungry, eyeing the monkeys] At first he was glad. 'Food has come to me,' he said. Then he heard them speak of Jatayu's death, and he cried out.
[Sampati telling his story] Long ago, he and Jatayu had raced toward the sun. Sampati spread his wings to shield his brother, and the sun burned them away.
[Sampati pointing across the sea] With his sharp vulture eyes he saw far across the ocean. 'I see Lanka,' he said, 'and a woman weeping in a garden.'
[Sampati's new wings growing] As he helped Rama's cause, new wings began to grow from his body, and he flew away, joyful.
[the monkeys staring at the endless sea] Lanka was a hundred yojanas away across the water. One said he could jump ten. Another, fifty. Angada could jump the distance, but not return.
[old Jambavan turning to Hanuman] Jambavan, the wise old bear, knew who could.
[Hanuman sitting silently apart] He turned to Hanuman, who sat alone, saying nothing.

## 39 | The Great Leap | One leap. One mission.
src: Valmiki, Kishkindha Kanda 66-67, Sundara Kanda 1
[baby Hanuman leaping at the sun] As a baby, Hanuman thought the rising sun was a ripe fruit, and leapt into the sky to eat it.
[Indra striking him with a thunderbolt] Indra struck him with his thunderbolt, and he fell. The gods blessed him with great powers.
[Hanuman sitting alone, forgetting his powers] But as a child, a curse from the sages had made Hanuman forget his own strength, until someone reminded him.
[Jambavan reminding him] Jambavan spoke of his birth, his speed and his courage. 'You are the son of the wind. Rise, Hanuman. The whole world waits for you.'
[Hanuman remembering, eyes shining] Slowly, Hanuman remembered. His body began to glow.
[Hanuman growing to a mountain's size] He grew vast as a mountain and stood on Mount Mahendra. The earth trembled. Rocks cracked. Animals ran from their caves.
[Hanuman bowing to the gods before the leap] He bowed to the sun, to Indra, to the wind, and to Rama.
[Hanuman leaping into the sky] 'Jai Shri Ram!' he roared, and leapt toward Lanka. The mountain sank under the force, and trees flew after him into the sky.

## 40 | Surasa and Simhika | Two tests passed.
src: Valmiki, Sundara Kanda 1
[Hanuman flying over the ocean] Hanuman flew over the ocean like a comet. The ocean god raised the golden mountain Mainaka from the waves to let him rest.
[Hanuman touching the mountain and flying on] Hanuman only touched it with his hand in thanks. 'I cannot rest until I find Sita,' he said.
[the sea-serpent Surasa rising with a huge open mouth] The gods tested Hanuman on his flight. First, the sea-serpent Surasa rose with a huge open mouth. 'No one passes without entering my mouth.'
[Hanuman growing larger, Surasa's mouth growing] Hanuman grew larger, and her mouth grew larger. Ten yojanas. Twenty. Ninety.
[Hanuman shrinking to a thumb's size] Then he shrank to the size of a thumb, flew in and slipped out through her jaws. She smiled and blessed him.
[the shadow-demoness Simhika reaching up from the water] Next, Simhika, a demoness who caught creatures by their shadows, grabbed his shadow and dragged him down.
[Hanuman tearing through her] Hanuman let himself fall into her mouth, entered her body and tore free.
[Hanuman flying on over the calm sea] The sea was clear. Ahead, on the horizon, golden Lanka began to shine.
